# Worked examples

Before and after, by document type. The examples are invented; no real people, meetings or figures.

Each one shows the same failure differently: the machine draft is not wrong, it is *unchosen*. Every phrase is the phrase that always goes there.

---

## 1. Declining a meeting request on the principal's behalf

**Before**

> Dear Mr Halloran,
>
> I hope this email finds you well.
>
> Thank you so much for reaching out regarding a potential meeting with Ms Okafor. I really appreciate your interest and your patience while I looked into this.
>
> Unfortunately, I regret to inform you that Ms Okafor's diary is extremely full at this moment in time, and she will not have the bandwidth to take a meeting in the coming weeks. I do hope you understand.
>
> Please don't hesitate to reach out in the future, and we will endeavour to find a suitable time.
>
> Warmest regards,

**After**

> Dear Mr Halloran,
>
> Thank you for the invitation. Ms Okafor won't be able to meet before the end of October, as her diary is committed through to the board meeting on the 28th.
>
> If it would help, I can put something in for early November, or she's happy for you to speak to Daniel Reyes in the meantime. He leads on this and I can introduce you.
>
> Best regards,

What changed: the decline moved to the first line instead of arriving in paragraph three behind an apology. A real reason and a real date replaced "at this moment in time". Two concrete alternatives replaced "we will endeavour to find a suitable time", which offers nothing. The warmth is now in the offer of help rather than in adjectives.

## 2. Chasing a signature

**Before**

> Hi Priya,
>
> Hope you're well! So sorry to chase again on this one.
>
> I just wanted to quickly check whether you might have had a chance to look at the signature page I sent over last week? No rush at all if not, I know how busy things are at the moment!
>
> Thanks so much in advance for your patience and understanding.

**After**

> Hi Priya,
>
> Following up on the signature page from last Tuesday. We need it back by Thursday to get the pack out to directors on Friday.
>
> Let me know if there's something holding it up and I'll sort it.

What changed: five hedges and two apologies removed. The chase now says what is needed, by when, and why the date exists, which is the information that actually gets a signature. The last line offers help rather than apologising for asking. Note it is not cold. "I'll sort it" is warmer than "thanks so much in advance", because it offers something.

## 3. Internal announcement

**Before**

> Team,
>
> We are absolutely thrilled to announce that Sam Achebe will be joining us as our new Head of Operations, commencing Monday 14 September.
>
> Sam brings a wealth of experience and a proven track record of driving operational excellence, and we are confident that her innovative approach and collaborative mindset will be a game-changer for the team. Sam is a testament to the calibre of talent we continue to attract.
>
> Please join us in extending a warm welcome to Sam!

**After**

> Team,
>
> Sam Achebe joins us on Monday 14 September as Head of Operations.
>
> She comes from Monzo, where she ran the same function for 4 years and rebuilt their incident process. She'll be sitting with the product team on the 3rd floor.
>
> There's coffee and cake in the kitchen at 11 on Monday if you want to say hello.

What changed: the puffery went, and specifics replaced it. "A wealth of experience" tells you nothing; "ran the same function at Monzo for 4 years" tells you what she can do. The last line is a real invitation rather than an instruction to feel welcoming. Shorter, and considerably warmer.

## 4. Briefing note for a principal

**Before**

> Ahead of your meeting with the Ferris Group tomorrow, I wanted to provide you with a comprehensive overview of the key contextual factors.
>
> It is important to note that the relationship has faced certain challenges over the past quarter. Furthermore, there have been some concerns raised regarding delivery timelines. That said, there remain significant opportunities for collaboration going forward.
>
> Please find attached the relevant documentation for your review.

**After**

> Ferris Group, tomorrow 10am, their offices.
>
> Two things to know. They've missed the last 3 delivery dates, most recently the March integration, which slipped 6 weeks. Marcus raised it with them in writing on 12 April and got a holding reply.
>
> They're also mid-way through their own restructure, and Elena Voss, your counterpart, may not be in the role by Q4.
>
> Pack attached. The delivery correspondence is at tab 3.

What changed: the abstractions became facts with dates attached. A principal reading the first version learns nothing they can act on. Notice this version is not casual. It is compressed, which is what a briefing note should be. Fragments are fine here.

## 5. Minutes, and where "humanising" goes wrong

**Before, machine-drafted**

> The Committee engaged in a robust discussion regarding the proposed budget increase. Following a comprehensive exchange of views, it was ultimately agreed that the matter would be revisited. The Chair emphasised the critical importance of fiscal discipline, underscoring the significance of the current climate. It was noted that further analysis serves as a necessary prerequisite.

**Over-corrected, wrong**

> Everyone had a proper go at each other about the budget. In the end they parked it. The Chair banged on about being careful with money, fair enough given where we are. Someone said we need more analysis first.

**Correct**

> The Committee discussed the proposed £340,000 budget increase. Ms Reyes supported it on the basis of the Q3 volume forecast. Mr Adeyemi opposed it, citing the same forecast's downside case. No decision was reached.
>
> The Chair asked for a fuller analysis of the downside case before the item returns.
>
> Action: prepare downside analysis for the October meeting. Owner: Ms Reyes. Due: 14 October.

What changed: the first version is padded ("robust discussion", "comprehensive exchange of views", "serves as a necessary prerequisite") and hides who said what behind the passive voice. The second strips the formality, which is the mistake this skill exists to prevent. Minutes are a record, and that version is unusable.

The third keeps the formal register and fixes the actual problems: names the speakers, states the figure, records that there was disagreement rather than smoothing it into "it was agreed", and turns the vague requirement into an owned action with a date. It is more formal than the machine draft and reads far more like a person.

## 6. Travel confirmation

**Before**

> Dear Ms Lindqvist,
>
> I hope this message finds you well. I am pleased to confirm that all arrangements for your upcoming trip to Singapore have now been finalised.
>
> Your flight has been booked and you will be departing on the evening of the fourteenth. Accommodation has been arranged at a centrally located hotel, and ground transportation will be provided. Please find your itinerary attached for your review.
>
> Should you require any further assistance, please do not hesitate to reach out.

**After**

> Singapore, 14 to 17 October. Full itinerary attached.
>
> **Out** SQ317, Thu 14 Oct, 22:05 from Heathrow T2. Car collects you at home 19:15.
> **Hotel** Fullerton Bay, 3 nights, harbour-side room. Booking ref FB4471932.
> **Back** SQ322, Sun 17 Oct, 01:30, lands 07:55 Monday.
>
> Your first meeting is 14:00 Friday, so you have the morning after landing.
>
> I've cleared Monday morning.

What changed: prose became scannable structure, because a traveller checks this in an airport queue, not in an armchair. Every vagueness became a fact: "evening of the fourteenth" is 22:05, "a centrally located hotel" is the Fullerton Bay with a booking reference. The last two lines are the assistant's judgement showing. They anticipated the two questions the principal would have asked.

---

## The pattern across all six

The machine draft is never grammatically wrong. It fails because nothing in it was chosen. Every phrase is the default phrase for that slot, and the specific facts that would make it useful are missing, hidden behind abstractions that sound professional.

The fix is almost always the same: **cut the phrase that always goes there, and put a fact in its place.**
