# The tells

Cut these unless the exact phrase is genuinely the right one. Every list here is about *formula*: the phrase that appears because it always appears, rather than because someone chose it.

Marketing AI-speak ("delve", "leverage", "unlock") barely shows up in support writing. What shows up instead is corporate-assistant boilerplate, which is older than AI but which AI reproduces perfectly. That is the tell: not that the words are new, but that they are the ones a template would pick.

## Openers

Cut and replace with the reason for writing:

- I hope this email finds you well
- I hope you're doing well / I hope you had a good weekend
- I hope this message finds you in good health
- I wanted to reach out regarding
- I'm reaching out to
- I'm writing to inform you that
- I wanted to touch base about
- Just circling back on
- Just following up to see
- I trust you are well
- Further to my below email
- As per my previous email / as per my last message
- Per our conversation
- I hope you don't mind me asking

Better: name the thing. "Thursday's board pack is attached." "Can we move Tuesday's 3pm?" "Quick one on the Frankfurt hotel."

A greeting is fine. A pleasantry that says nothing is not. If you want warmth in the opener, make it specific: "Hope Lisbon went well" beats "hope you're well", because only a person who knows them could write it.

## Sign-offs and closers

- Please don't hesitate to reach out / to contact me
- Please feel free to reach out with any questions
- Thank you for your patience and understanding
- Thank you in advance for your cooperation
- I appreciate your understanding on this matter
- Looking forward to hearing from you soon
- I look forward to your prompt response
- Should you have any questions, please do not hesitate
- Thanks so much in advance!
- Warmest regards (unless it is genuinely how you sign)

Better: "Let me know if anything's unclear." "Shout if you'd rather do it differently." Or nothing. A great many emails need no closing line at all.

## Stacked hedges

Each of these is fine alone and occasionally right. Stacked, they are the clearest sign of a machine draft, and they make an ask so soft the recipient does not register it as one.

just · quickly · possibly · perhaps · maybe · might · I was wondering if · I wanted to see whether · if you get a chance · if that's okay · no worries if not · sorry to bother you · apologies for the extra email · I don't want to add to your plate

- Bad: "I just wanted to quickly check whether you might possibly have a moment to look at this, no rush at all if not!"
- Good: "Could you look at this before Thursday?"

One softener is a courtesy. Three is an apology for existing.

## Reflexive apology

- So sorry to chase
- Apologies for the delay in coming back to you
- Sorry for the many emails
- Sorry to be a pain
- I hate to ask again but

Chasing is the job. Do it plainly: "Following up on the signed copy. We need it by Friday for the board pack." Keep a genuine apology for a genuine error, where it should be specific and unhedged.

## Filler transitions

Furthermore · Additionally · Moreover · That said · That being said · With that in mind · On top of that · It is important to note that · It is worth noting that · Please be advised that · Kindly note that · In order to · At this moment in time · Going forward · Moving forward · At the end of the day

Use a real connector or start a new sentence.

## Corporate abstraction

Cut where a plain word exists:

- reach out → email, call, ask
- touch base → talk, meet, check
- circle back → follow up, come back to
- loop in → copy in, tell
- bandwidth → time, capacity
- align on → agree
- take this offline → discuss separately
- action (as a verb) → do, handle
- socialise (an idea) → share, discuss
- leverage → use
- utilise → use
- facilitate → arrange, run, help
- commence → start
- endeavour → try
- ascertain → find out, check
- in receipt of → have, received
- at your earliest convenience → by \[date\], or "when you can"
- please find attached → attached is, I've attached
- kindly (as a request softener) → please, or nothing

"At your earliest convenience" deserves singling out. It sounds courteous and means nothing enforceable. If it matters by Thursday, say Thursday.

## Marketing register in the wrong place

These belong in a brochure and appear in internal announcements written by AI:

delighted to announce · thrilled to share · excited to share · we are pleased to inform you · a valued member of the team · an exciting opportunity · in this ever-evolving landscape · we remain committed to · testament to · showcase · seamless · robust · streamline · empower · elevate · unlock · game-changer · best-in-class · reach out to learn more

An internal announcement should read as though a person is pleased, which is a different thing from a press release. "Sam starts on Monday and will be sitting with the product team. She's come from Monzo, where she ran the same function." That is warmer than "We are thrilled to announce."

## Fake emphasis

- This is a crucial reminder
- Please note the importance of
- It cannot be overstated
- This is a critical action item
- As a matter of urgency (unless it genuinely is)
- Highlighting the importance of / underscoring the significance of

State the fact and the deadline. The reader decides how urgent it is, and a person who marks everything urgent stops being believed.

## Structural tells

**Rule of three.** Every claim arriving as three items ("clear, timely and accurate") when the truth is two of them, or four. Say the number of things that are true.

**Bulleted fragments that should be a sentence.** Three bullets of four words each is usually one sentence broken up to look organised.

**Restating the question.** "Regarding your question about the venue, the venue is confirmed." Just answer.

**The summary that repeats the email.** A four-line email does not need a closing recap.

**Meta-commentary.** "Please see below for details." "I've outlined the options below." Show the options.

**Elegant variation.** Renaming the same person or thing to avoid repeating a word: "Priya" then "the finance lead" then "she". Use the name again. In minutes this is not just a style problem, it creates genuine ambiguity about who did what.

**Negative parallelism.** "This isn't a scheduling problem, it's a priorities problem." Rejecting one frame to elevate another creates a feeling of insight without adding information. Say the positive claim directly: "The priorities are unclear." Allowed only when correcting a specific factual error: "The meeting is Tuesday, not Thursday."

## Punctuation and typography

- **Em dashes.** Cut them. Full stop, comma, colon, semicolon or brackets.
- **Exclamation marks.** At most one per email, and only where genuine. AI drafts scatter them to signal friendliness, which reads as nervous.
- **Ellipses** to trail off in professional writing. Finish the sentence.
- **Quotation marks for emphasis.** They read as sarcasm.
- **ALL CAPS** for urgency. Say it in words.
- **Bold on four things in one paragraph.** Emphasises nothing.

## In minutes and formal records specifically

Different failure modes, because the register is meant to be formal:

- **Characterising rather than recording.** "The proposal was unfortunately rejected." Minutes record. Drop the adverb.
- **Manufactured consensus.** "It was agreed" where there was an argument. If the record shows agreement that did not exist, it will fail the person relying on it.
- **Passive voice hiding the actor.** "It was decided" where you know who decided. Name them.
- **Inflated verbs.** "Serves as", "stands as", "represents a", "plays a role in". Use is, has, does.
- **Discussion sections that transcribe.** If a paragraph reproduces the back-and-forth, it is a transcript, not a minute. Summarise the substance.
- **Inferred deadlines and owners.** Not a style problem, an accuracy one, and the most common way minutes cause trouble. "No date set" is the honest entry.

## Words that are fine

Do not remove a word just because it appears on a list somewhere. If "escalate" describes what happened, use it. If "confidential" is the accurate word, use it. Precision is not a tell. Formula is.
