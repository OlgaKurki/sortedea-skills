# Question bank

The interview, section by section. Ask in the user's own words — these are prompts for you, not a script to read aloud. Tier is marked on each group so you know where the answer goes.

**Pacing:** one section per exchange. Write the section to the file before moving on.

**Cold-start priority:** Sections 2, 3, 4, 5 first. Sections 1, 7, 9 need standing you may not have yet in week one — ask for those once the principal trusts you with them, and leave visible gaps until then.

---

## 1. Personal profile — Tier 2 (annex), pointer only in the operating file

- Full name as it should appear in formal documents, and what they're called day to day
- Preferred name and pronunciation, if either is not obvious
- Date of birth *(annex — often needed for bookings and visas; never in the operating file)*
- Contact numbers: mobile, office, and which one is acceptable out of hours *(the "which one, when" rule is Tier 1; the numbers are Tier 2)*
- Emergency contacts and the order to call them
- Family and close personal connections you may need to recognise or route calls from
- Addresses: primary residence, secondary residences, and which is the default for deliveries
- Properties, if relevant to their logistics

**Ask into the operating file instead:** "If someone from the family calls, what's the rule?" — the *rule* is Tier 1 and useful. The names and numbers are Tier 2.

---

## 2. Work profile — Tier 1

- Formal title, and how they want to be described externally
- Direct reports: names, roles, and how the principal prefers you to interact with each
- Who else has a standing claim on their time — board, co-founders, key investors, key clients
- Meeting style: do they want pre-reads or a verbal walk-in? Discussion or decision? Do they read in the room?
- Reporting preferences: what format, what cadence, how much detail. Bullet summary or full document?
- How they make decisions — fast and verbal, or slow and written? Do they want options with a recommendation, or options neutral?
- What they consider a waste of their time
- What they always want to be asked about before it happens

---

## 3. Daily routine — Tier 1

- Working hours, and the honest version versus the official version
- Morning routine and when they're actually reachable
- Peak thinking time — the block to defend
- Lunch: usual time, whether it's a working slot or a real break, dietary requirements, go-to places
- Evening wrap-up: do they want an end-of-day summary, a next-day preview, or silence after a certain hour?
- Weekend and holiday rules — what reaches them, what waits
- Time zones they routinely work across

---

## 4. Scheduling guidelines — Tier 1

- Default meeting length, and whether they want buffers between
- Preferred times and dead zones
- Format preference: in person, video, phone — and does it change by counterparty seniority?
- Non-negotiable standing commitments
- What can be moved, and who has authority to move it
- How much notice they need for a new commitment
- Travel buffer rules — how long before a flight, how soon after landing
- **The gatekeeping question:** who gets straight through, who gets routed to someone else, who gets a polite deferral. Ask this explicitly; it's the highest-value line in the playbook and the one most often left implicit.
- Recovery rules — after long-haul, after a board meeting, after an intense week

---

## 5. Communication guidelines — Tier 1

- Preferred channel by urgency: what's a call, what's a message, what's an email, what waits for the next 1:1
- Response time expectations they hold, and the ones they hold you to
- Their writing voice: formal or plain, long or short, warm or clipped. **Collect three or four real examples of their own writing and keep them with the playbook** — this is worth more than any description of their tone.
- Words, phrases and sign-offs they use; ones they dislike
- What you may send in their name without checking, what needs approval, what they always write themselves
- Language(s), and any conventions when writing to particular regions or counterparties
- How they want to be interrupted when something genuinely can't wait
- Your own tone with them — do they want brevity, or context?

---

## 6. Travel and logistics — Tier 1 for preferences, Tier 2 for numbers and payment

- Preferred airlines and alliance, and any they refuse
- Cabin class by route length; seat preference (specific — "aisle, forward of the wing, not by the galley" beats "aisle")
- Connections: tolerated or avoided? Minimum connection time they're comfortable with
- Departure timing habits — night before, first flight, never before X
- Lounge access and preferences
- Hotels: chains, specific properties by city, room type, floor, bed, quiet-side requests
- Ground transport: car service, ride-hailing, self-drive, and who books it
- Transfer preferences on frequent routes — write these per route, they're the ones you'll reuse
- Packing habits and what they always need in the bag
- Loyalty programme numbers *(annex)*
- Passport, visa status, and renewal dates *(annex — but keep the renewal reminder in the operating file, it's a date not an identifier)*
- Roaming and connectivity setup
- Travel payment method and approval route *(annex for the details; the approval route is Tier 1)*
- What a good itinerary looks like to them — one page? Calendar entries? Both?

---

## 7. Key relationships and networks — Tier 1 for the map, Tier 2 for personal contacts

- The people whose emails always matter, and why
- Board members, investors, key clients, mentors — and the state of each relationship
- Internal relationships that need care — who has history with whom
- Who the principal will always take a call from
- Who to route to someone else, and to whom
- Assistants and gatekeepers on the other side — often the most useful contacts you have
- Favourite vendors and providers: restaurants, florists, gift sources, tailor, stylist
- Personal contacts *(annex)*
- Dates that matter: anniversaries of the business, key relationship milestones, birthdays worth marking

---

## 8. Preferences and favourites — Tier 1

- Coffee and tea order, precisely
- Food likes, dislikes, allergies and requirements *(record dietary requirements as operational fact — a restaurant needs them. Anything that is a medical condition rather than a preference belongs in the annex.)*
- Go-to restaurants by occasion: client lunch, team dinner, quiet conversation, celebration
- Drinks, and whether they drink at work events
- Gifts they've given and gifts that landed well — build this list over time, it's the hardest thing to reconstruct later
- Sport, culture, hobbies — the things worth flagging when tickets or invitations come up
- Small things that make a bad day better. Write them down. This is the line that makes a playbook feel like it was written by someone who was paying attention.

---

## 9. Crisis and emergency plans — Tier 2, with Tier 1 procedure

- Medical contacts: doctor, dentist, specialists *(annex)*
- Preferred hospital and clinic by location *(annex)*
- Insurance and assistance-line details *(annex)*
- **Tier 1 procedure — this part belongs in the operating file:** who you call first if something happens on a trip; who in the business must be told and in what order; who covers their commitments; what you're authorised to cancel without asking.

Health information about a named person is among the most sensitive material an assistant holds. Keep it in the annex, keep the annex out of AI tools, and record only what you'd actually need in an emergency.

---

## 10. Tech and tools — Tier 1 for platforms, Tier 3 for access

- Devices they use and which one they actually read email on
- Preferred meeting platform, and any they can't use
- Tools they live in, and tools they refuse
- How they take notes, and whether they want yours
- File format preferences — do they want a PDF, a doc, or the text in the email body?
- How they consume long documents: printed, tablet, or summarised
- **Access:** record only *where* credentials live and who holds delegated access. Never the credentials. See the Tier 3 rules in `sensitivity-tiers.md`.

---

## 11. Confidentiality protocols — Tier 1

- What categories of information never leave the office
- What must never be discussed in an open-plan space or on a call in public
- Media policy: who may speak to press, what you do with an inbound request
- Document handling: what gets shared how, what must be encrypted, what never goes to personal email
- What may and may not be put into AI tools — **get an explicit answer to this and write it down.** It protects both of you, and most principals have never been asked.
- Who else in the organisation is cleared for what
- What to do if you're asked for something by someone senior who shouldn't have it
