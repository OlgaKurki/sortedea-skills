# What to count

Count these across 25 to 40 pieces. Do not estimate. The gap between what you believe about your writing and what you do is the whole reason for this step.

Keep a tally and collect verbatim examples as you go.

## Shape

- Mean sentence length in words. Calculate it separately for messages, email and documents.
- Longest and shortest sentence you can find. The range matters as much as the average.
- Do you use fragments? Where?
- Mean paragraph length in sentences. Blocks, or one-liners?

## Openings

- Greeting variants with counts. "Hi Sarah" 14, "Sarah," 6, no greeting 5.
- Do you use the person's name?
- First sentence: the point, a pleasantry, or context?
- **Count how many emails open with the ask.** This single number tells you more about your register than any adjective.

## Closings

- Sign-off variants with counts.
- Name, initials, or nothing?
- Is there a closing line, or does the last real sentence end it?

## Vocabulary

- **The 20 words and phrases that recur and are not common English.** This is the fingerprint.
- Your word for common things. Call or catch-up or sync. Deck or presentation.
- Jargon you use naturally, and jargon you avoid.
- The word that appears in almost everything. Everyone has one. Yours is invisible to you, so this is where the two people you asked in step 3 earn their place.
- Spelling and conventions. Organise or organize. Date format. 24-hour or am/pm.

## Mechanics

- Contraction rate. Do you write "don't" or "do not"? Does it shift with formality?
- **"I" against "we".** Count the ratio, and check whether it changes when the news is bad. People hide behind "we".
- Active or passive, especially when delivering something unwelcome.
- Exclamation marks per 1,000 words.
- Question marks: do you ask or instruct?
- Em dashes, semicolons, ellipses, brackets. Note the ones you never use.
- Emoji: never, rarely, or a specific small set?

## Structure

- Bullets or prose?
- Bold or underline for emphasis?
- Where the ask sits: first line, last line, or buried?
- Typical length by message type.
- Reply inline, top-post, or rewrite?

## Behaviour

These make a draft convincing.

- **How you say no.** Collect every decline verbatim. Reason given? Alternative offered? Apology?
- **How you chase.** Reference the earlier message, restate the deadline, escalate?
- **How you ask for something.** Imperative, question, or hedged into invisibility?
- **How you disagree.** Directly, by question, or by going quiet and picking up the phone?
- **How you apologise.** Count how often, and whether the thing warranted it.
- **How you praise.** Public or private, specific or general.
- **What earns a one-line reply** against a full one.

## Register shifts

Small table. For each pair, note what changes.

- To a peer, against to someone senior
- Internal, against external
- Good news, against bad
- Considered, against typed on a phone
- Someone you know well, against a first contact

The differences are the profile. One voice description produces drafts that are wrong half the time.

## Absence

List what never appears anywhere. Absence across 30 samples beats anything you could tell yourself.

Look for: exclamation marks, emoji, apologies, hedges, superlatives, questions, humour, greetings, sign-offs, first-person plural.

## Samples to keep

Pull 15 to 20 short verbatim examples, stripped of names, figures and confidential detail:

- 3 openings across registers
- 2 declines
- 2 chases
- 2 pieces of bad news
- 2 short replies
- 1 long considered piece
- 1 piece of praise
- Anything that made you think "that is very me"

The samples do more work than the rules.
