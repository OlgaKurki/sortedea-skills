# Gap questions

Roughly 20 questions, all about things a corpus cannot show.

Ask the principal what you can. Answer the rest yourself, from observation. Mark each answer **[asked]** or **[observed]**, because the observed ones are where you will turn out to be wrong.

Do not run this as an interrogation. Most of it can be picked up in ordinary conversation over a fortnight, and you will get better answers that way.

## For the principal, if you can get 15 minutes

1. Do you write your own LinkedIn posts, or does someone help? *Ask this first. It decides whether a third of your corpus is usable.*
2. Is there anything you would never put in an email, and always pick up the phone for instead?
3. What do you not want to sound like?
4. Is there a word or phrase you dislike seeing in something signed by you?
5. When I have drafted for you before, what have you found yourself changing?
6. Who do you write differently for, and how?
7. When you say no to someone, do you want a reason given, or is a clean no better?
8. Are there topics you will not comment on publicly, at all?
9. How do you want to sound when something has gone wrong?
10. What may I send in your name without checking, and what must always come to you first?

Question 5 is the most valuable and the one people answer least well in the abstract. If they struggle, show them two of your old drafts next to what they sent instead, and ask what they changed and why. The answer to that is worth more than the other nine.

## For you, from observation

11. What have they told you to stop doing? Every past correction is a rule.
12. What is the tell that they are annoyed? Shorter sentences, no greeting, one-word replies, or the switch from "we" to "I"?
13. What do they write themselves that they could delegate? That is where they care most about voice.
14. Which of their emails have caused a problem, and what was wrong with it?
15. Where do they adjust their voice in a way that surprised you when you noticed it?
16. What would embarrass them if it went out under their name?
17. Do they read their own drafts aloud, print them, or send immediately?
18. Is there anyone whose writing they admire, or have asked you to imitate?
19. What do they never say, that people in their position usually say?
20. If a colleague read a draft you wrote, what would give away that it was not them?

Question 20 is the one to keep asking yourself. When a draft comes back edited, the answer changes, and the profile gets better.

## Two things not to record

**Their view on how they write, taken at face value.** Ask it by all means, and write it down as what they believe. Then check it against the corpus. When the two disagree, the corpus is right. People describe the writer they intend to be.

**Anything about them personally that does not change how you draft.** This is a voice profile, not a file on a person. If a fact would not alter a sentence, leave it out.
